📦 Before You Leave Reverb 4 tasks ▼
Export your Reverb sales history
Save a record of everything you've sold so far
details

Get a copy of your past sales before you change anything. Reverb has a built-in export:

  1. Log into Reverb and open your Orders page in the seller dashboard.
  2. Set a Start Date and End Date. Go back as far as your shop goes.
  3. Click Export in the top right, then Orders.
  4. When it's ready, click Download Export and save the CSV somewhere safe.

You get order numbers, buyer names and addresses, SKUs, prices, and shipping. Keep it for your records and for warranty questions down the road. (Reverb's help page on this)

Pedal builder tip Run this again right before you close or pause your Reverb shop so the file covers everything.
Save your listings and photos
Back up every description and image you've put on Reverb
details

Your photos and descriptions took real work. Don't count on them staying on Reverb forever.

  • Listings CSV: In Reverb, go to Manage Inventory > Export Your Listings. Reverb emails you a download link (it expires, so grab it quickly). This needs at least 10 completed sales. If you're not there yet, email Reverb's integrations team and ask them to turn it on. (Reverb's CSV guide)
  • Photos: Reverb doesn't have a bulk photo download. Either right-click and save each one, or skip it and use an import app (next task), which brings the photos over for you.
Pedal builder tip If you still have the original photos on your phone or camera, use those on your new store. They'll be sharper than anything you pull back off a website.
Bring your listings into Shopify
Import them with an app instead of retyping everything
details

If you have more than a handful of listings, don't rebuild them by hand. Once your Shopify store exists (see the Shopify section below), install an import app:

  1. SyncGear is a Shopify app built for Reverb. It pulls your Reverb listings into Shopify, photos included. It's free for up to 25 one-time imports. Paid plans ($14.99/month and up) add ongoing inventory syncing.
  2. Install it, connect your Reverb account, pick the listings, and import.
  3. Go through each imported product afterward. Check the price, photos, and description, and fix anything that came over weird.

Got just a few pedals? Typing them in by hand is fine. See "Add your products" below.

Decide: keep Reverb or go all-in on Shopify?
You can sell on both, but there are trade-offs
details

You have two options:

  • Option A: Sell on both. Keep your Reverb listings up and sell on Shopify too. More visibility while you build your own traffic.
  • Option B: Go all-in on Shopify. Close your Reverb shop and send everyone to your site. Simpler, but you lose Reverb's built-in buyers.

The math: Reverb takes a 5% selling fee plus 3.19% + $0.49 for payment processing, both charged on the total including shipping. That's roughly 8.5% of every sale. Shopify Payments on the Basic plan is 2.9% + 30¢ per order, plus your monthly plan.

Most builders start with Option A and shift more sales to their own store over time. If you do sell on both, Reverb's free Shopify integration lets Shopify run the show: you manage inventory in Shopify and it updates Reverb. It matches products by SKU, so give every pedal a unique SKU. When one sells on one platform, it doesn't stay listed on the other.

Pedal builder tip Keep your prices the same on both. Nobody likes finding out they paid more than the next guy.
🌐 Get Your Domain Name 3 tasks ▼
Choose your domain name
Pick a web address that matches your brand
details

Your domain name is your web address, like nokillpedal.co. Here's how to pick a good one:

  • Keep it short and easy to spell.
  • Match your brand name if you can.
  • .com is the most common, but .co or .shop work too.
  • Skip hyphens and numbers. They're hard to say out loud.

Test it by saying it to someone: "Check out my site at ___." If they can spell it without asking, you're good.

Pedal builder tip Check that the name is free on Instagram and Facebook too. You want the same name everywhere.
Register your domain (~$10-20/year)
Buy your web address from a domain registrar
details

A registrar is just a company that sells web addresses. Good options:

  • Through Shopify: The easiest. Buy it inside your Shopify admin (Settings > Domains) and it connects itself. Starts around $9-15/year depending on the ending.
  • Namecheap: Popular and cheap, with good support.
  • Cloudflare Registrar: Sells domains at cost with no markup. A bit more technical.

If you don't want to deal with any setup, buy through Shopify. It also unlocks Shopify's free email forwarding (next section), which only works on domains Shopify manages.

Pedal builder tip Turn on auto-renew. If your domain expires, someone else can grab it.
Connect your domain to Shopify
Point your web address to your new store
details

Bought it through Shopify? Skip this. It's already connected.

Bought it somewhere else? You need to point it at Shopify:

  1. In Shopify, go to Settings > Domains > Connect existing domain.
  2. Type in your domain and click Next.
  3. Shopify shows you DNS settings. DNS is the internet's address book: it tells browsers where your site lives.
  4. Log into your registrar and find its DNS settings.
  5. Copy in the records Shopify gave you.
  6. Save and wait. It can take up to 48 hours but usually works within a few.
Pedal builder tip If this feels confusing, buy the domain through Shopify instead. A few extra bucks a year is worth zero hassle.
📧 Set Up Your Email 2 tasks ▼
Set up a professional email address
Get an email like you@yourbrand.com
details

An address like hello@yourbrand.com looks more legit than a Gmail. Options:

  • Shopify forwarding (free): Mail sent to your domain lands in your personal inbox. Set it up in Settings > Domains > your domain > Email forwarding. This only works if Shopify manages your domain. It won't forward to Yahoo or AOL addresses.
  • Google Workspace: Full Gmail on your own domain, about $7/month per person on a yearly plan. Best if you want to send AND receive as your brand.
  • Zoho Mail: Its free plan covers up to 5 people on one domain. The catch: you use Zoho's website or app, not the Gmail or Apple Mail app you already have.
Pedal builder tip Start with free forwarding. Upgrade to Google Workspace once replying from your brand address actually matters to you.

🛒 Build Your Shopify Store 14 tasks ▼
Create your Shopify account
Sign up and start your free trial
details

Go to Shopify and sign up. Right now new stores get 3 days free, then $1/month for the first 3 months.

  1. Click Start free trial.
  2. Enter your email and store name.
  3. Answer the setup questions. Pick "I'm just starting" if it asks.
  4. You'll land on your dashboard, which Shopify calls the "admin."

The Basic plan is all you need to start: $39/month, or $29/month if you pay for a year up front.

Pedal builder tip The $1 months give you plenty of time to build everything before you're paying full price. No need to rush to launch.
Pick and customize your theme
Choose how your store looks
details

A theme controls how your store looks. Shopify's free ones are good.

  1. In your admin, go to Online Store > Themes.
  2. New stores start on Horizon. It's clean and works for a pedal shop as-is.
  3. Want a different look? Browse the free themes. Horizon has sister themes (Atelier, Heritage, Ritual, and others) built the same way.
  4. Click Customize to start editing.

Don't overthink it. You can switch later. Pick something clean and start adding content.

Pedal builder tip A clean, fast store with great photos beats a fancy, cluttered one every time.
Add your products
Create a listing for each pedal you sell
details

If you imported from Reverb, just check each product over. Otherwise, for each pedal:

  1. Go to Products > Add product.
  2. Add the title (the pedal name).
  3. Write a description: what it sounds like, who it's for, what makes it different.
  4. Upload your photos. The first one is the main image.
  5. Set the price and a SKU (a short unique code, like WMR-001).
  6. Set inventory. Track quantity if you build in batches, or uncheck "Track quantity" if you build to order.
  7. Add the weight (boxed) for shipping rates.
  8. Click Save.
Pedal builder tip Shopify's built-in AI (Shopify Magic) can draft a description for you. Use it to get unstuck, then rewrite it the way you'd actually talk. "This thing cleans up great with your volume knob" beats "Features unity gain bypass architecture."
Create collections
Group your pedals into categories
details

Collections are groups of products, like "Overdrives," "Fuzz," or "All Pedals." They help people browse.

  1. Go to Products > Collections.
  2. Click Create collection.
  3. Name it (e.g., "Overdrive Pedals").
  4. Choose Manual so you hand-pick which products go in.
  5. Add the products.
  6. Make at least an "All Pedals" collection plus one per category.
Add tags to your products
Tags help with filtering and organization
details

Tags are labels that help you sort and filter products, e.g., "overdrive," "fuzz," "germanium," "under-200."

  1. Open a product in your admin.
  2. Find Tags on the right side.
  3. Type a tag and press Enter. Add a few per product.
  4. Click Save.

Stay consistent: always "overdrive," never sometimes "OD."

Set up your navigation menus
Create the links at the top and bottom of your site
details

Navigation is the menu at the top of your store.

  1. Type Menus into the search bar at the top of your admin and open it.
  2. Click Main menu.
  3. Add links to your collections ("All Pedals," "Overdrive"), your About page, and your Contact page.
  4. Keep it to 4-6 links.

Then set up the Footer menu with Shipping Policy, Return Policy, Contact, and FAQ.

Design your collection pages
Make your product grid look good
details

Collection pages show a grid of products.

  1. Go to Online Store > Themes > Customize.
  2. Use the page picker at the top to switch to a collection page.
  3. Set how many products show per row.
  4. Keep images the same shape. Square works best in a grid.
Pedal builder tip Shoot every pedal on the same background with the same light. A cheap photo light box makes this easy and makes even a three-pedal store look pro.
Polish your product pages
Make each pedal page convincing
details

Every product page is a sales pitch:

  • Multiple photos: front, back, side, guts shot, on a board.
  • Clear description: what it sounds like, what it stacks well with, what makes it yours.
  • Specs: power (9V center-negative, current draw), size, bypass type.
  • Demo video: embed a YouTube demo with the video button in the description editor.
Pedal builder tip Show the guts. Pedal people love clean wiring and good parts. It proves you care about the build, not just the paint job.
Build your homepage
Create a welcoming front page
details

Most visitors see your homepage first. Keep it focused:

  1. Go to Online Store > Themes > Customize.
  2. Add a big hero image: your best pedal or your bench.
  3. Add a featured collection showing your pedals.
  4. Add a short About blurb: who you are and why you build.
  5. Stop at 3-4 sections.
Pedal builder tip People buying from your own site want to know who's behind the pedals. A short, honest About section goes a long way.
Set up payments
Connect Shopify Payments so you can get paid
details

Tell Shopify where to send your money:

  1. Go to Settings > Payments.
  2. Activate Shopify Payments.
  3. Enter your business info, bank account, and tax details.
  4. That turns on credit cards, Apple Pay, Google Pay, and Shop Pay.

On the Basic plan, US cards cost 2.9% + 30¢ per order, with no extra Shopify transaction fee.

Pedal builder tip Turn on PayPal too. Some buyers won't check out without it, and it's a two-minute setup on the same page.
Configure shipping rates
Set up how much you charge for shipping
details

Go to Settings > Shipping and delivery. Your options:

  • Flat rate: Same charge on every order (e.g., $8). Simple and predictable.
  • Free shipping: Build the cost into the price. People love "free shipping."
  • Calculated rates: Shopify works it out from weight and destination. Accurate, but it can surprise people at checkout.

Most small builders do best with flat rate or free shipping.

Pedal builder tip If your pedals are all about the same size, weigh one boxed up, check the USPS Ground Advantage price, and set a flat rate from that.
Get set up to ship
Labels, a scale, and boxes so orders go out fast
details

On Reverb, buying labels was built in. On Shopify it is too: open an order and click Create shipping label. Shopify Shipping gets you discounted USPS and UPS rates and fills in the address for you.

A few cheap things make shipping way faster:

  • A shipping scale: Weigh the box so you don't overpay (or underpay) on postage.
  • A 4x6 thermal label printer: No ink, no tape, no cutting paper labels. Worth it once you're shipping a few a week.
  • Mailer boxes: Find one size that fits your pedals with padding and buy it in bulk.

Prefer to buy labels outside Shopify? Pirate Ship is free and gets similar discounted rates.

Create essential pages
Add About, Contact, Shipping Policy, and Return Policy pages
details

Every store needs a few basic pages. Go to Online Store > Pages and create:

  • About: Your story, why you build, a photo of you or your bench.
  • Contact: Pick the "contact" page template to get Shopify's built-in form.
  • FAQ: Power supply, warranty, build times, international shipping.

For the legal stuff, go to Settings > Policies. Open each one (Refund, Shipping, Privacy, Terms) and click Insert template for a starting draft. Edit it to match how you actually do things.

Pedal builder tip Be clear about returns. A lot of builders do "no returns on custom orders, 14 days on stock pedals."
Place a test order
Make sure everything works before going live
details

Buy something from your own store before anyone else does. If you're using Shopify Payments:

  1. Go to Settings > Payments and click Manage on Shopify Payments.
  2. Check Enable test mode and save.
  3. Go to your store, add a pedal to the cart, and check out with the test card 4242 4242 4242 4242 (any future expiration date, any 3-digit code).
  4. Make sure the order shows up and you get the confirmation email.
  5. Turn test mode back off. Real customers can't pay while it's on.

Not on Shopify Payments? Use the Test payment gateway instead. Find it under Settings > Payments > See all other providers.

🚀 Boost Your Instagram Posts 5 tasks ▼
Understand what boosting is
Boosting = paying to show your post to more people
details

Boosting is the simplest kind of Instagram ad:

  • Pick a post you've already published.
  • Pay to show it to people who don't follow you yet.
  • Set where, roughly who, and how much to spend.
  • It runs for the days you choose, then stops on its own.
How much? You set the budget. A few dollars a day for a few days is enough to learn something. Most builders start around $20-50 per boost.
Pick the right post to boost
Not every post is worth boosting
details

Boost posts that already did well without paying. Look for one that:

  • Got more likes or comments than usual.
  • Shows the pedal clearly.
  • Says what to do next ("Available now, link in bio").
  • Is a demo Reel or a really strong product shot.
Pedal builder tip 15-30 second demo Reels usually boost best. Guitarists stop scrolling when they hear a good tone.
Step-by-step: boost a post
Follow these exact steps to set up your first boost
details
  1. Open the post in Instagram and tap Boost.
  2. Goal: Pick More website visits and set the link to your pedal's product page. A product page works better than your homepage.
  3. Audience: Instagram now uses Advantage+ audience by default. Here's what still works:
    • Location and minimum age are hard limits. Set location to where you ship and minimum age to 18.
    • Interests like "guitar," "effects units," and "electric guitar" are now only suggestions. Instagram uses them as a starting point and then shows the post to whoever it thinks will click.
  4. Budget: Start with $5/day for 5 days ($25 total).
  5. Review and tap Boost post.
Pedal builder tip Your first boost is a test. Spend $25, see what happens, adjust, and try again. Don't drop $100 on the first one.
Read your boost results
Understand what the numbers mean
details

Tap View insights on the boosted post:

  • Reach: How many different people saw it.
  • Link clicks: How many tapped through to your store. This is the number that matters.
  • Cost per click: What each store visit cost you. Under $1 is great, under $2 is fine.
  • Engagement: Likes, comments, saves. Nice, but clicks pay the bills.
Quick math: $25 for 30 clicks is about 83¢ a click. If 1 of those 30 people buys a $150 pedal, you spent $25 to make a $150 sale.
Common boosting mistakes to avoid
Learn from other builders' mistakes
details
  • Boosting a bad post. If it didn't grab anyone for free, paying won't fix it.
  • Picking "More profile visits." You want store visits. Choose "More website visits."
  • Fighting the targeting. You can't fence Instagram in with interests anymore. Make a post guitarists will stop for and let the algorithm find them.
  • Judging it in a day. Let a boost run its full length before deciding.
  • Quitting after one. Try 3-4 different posts before you decide boosting isn't for you.
Pedal builder tip Keep a simple log for each boost: which post, how much you spent, clicks, sales. After a few you'll see what works.
🔗 Connect Shopify to Instagram 5 tasks ▼
Install the Facebook & Instagram sales channel
Free Shopify app that connects your store to Meta
details

Meta owns Facebook and Instagram. This free channel links your store to both. Before you start, you need:

  • A Facebook Page for your brand. Even if you never post there, make a basic one with your name and logo.
  • Your Instagram Business account linked to that Page.

Understand what the pixel does
It tracks visitors so you can advertise to them later
details

The Meta Pixel is a bit of tracking code that the channel adds to your store for you. In plain English:

  • Someone visits your store and the pixel notes "this person was here."
  • They look at a pedal and it notes which one.
  • They add to cart or buy and it notes that too.
  • Later, your boosts and ads can be shown to those exact people.

That's called retargeting: showing ads to people who already showed interest. It works way better than ads to strangers.

Do I have to code anything? No. Shopify handles all of it. You never touch the code.
Turn up data sharing and check it's working
Make sure the tracking is actually running on your store
details
  1. In Shopify, open Facebook & Instagram > Settings > Data sharing settings.
  2. Set it to Maximum. This adds Meta's Conversions API, which keeps tracking working when browsers block the pixel.
  3. For a second check, install Meta's free Chrome extension, Meta Ads Data Advisor (it used to be called Pixel Helper).
  4. Visit your store. The extension should show your pixel firing.
Tag your pedals in posts
Let people tap a pedal in a post and land on its page
details

Once the channel is connected and Meta approves your shop (usually a few days), your Shopify products show up in Instagram.

  1. When you make a post or Reel, tap Tag products.
  2. Pick the pedal from your catalog.
  3. Anyone who taps the tag lands on that pedal's page in your store, where they check out.

Checkout used to happen inside Instagram. Meta phased that out, so every tagged sale now goes through your own store.

Set up retargeting audiences
Create groups of people to show ads to later
details

With the pixel collecting data, you can build "audiences": groups of people based on what they did on your site.

  1. Open Meta Ads Manager and go to Audiences.
  2. Click Create audience > Custom audience > Website.
  3. Make these three:
    • All visitors, 30 days
    • Viewed a product, 14 days
    • Added to cart, 7 days: people who almost bought. These are your hottest leads.

They'll be tiny at first. That's normal. They grow as your traffic does.

Pedal builder tip Retargeting runs from Ads Manager, not the Boost button. When you're ready, run a small ad to the "added to cart" audience showing the pedal they almost bought. It wins back a surprising number of sales.
💰 Increase Your Sales 7 tasks ▼
Set up email marketing
Send emails to past customers and subscribers
details

Email is one of the best ways to sell to people who already like you. Shopify's built-in tool, now called Shopify Messaging, gives you 10,000 free emails a month.

  1. In your admin, go to Marketing and create an email campaign. Shopify will have you install Shopify Messaging the first time.
  2. Pick a template and add your logo and colors.
  3. Make sure your theme's newsletter signup is turned on, usually in the footer.
  4. Turn on the free abandoned checkout email. It reminds people who got to checkout and stopped.

What to send: new pedal launches, limited runs and restocks, and build updates from the bench.

Pedal builder tip Every week or two is plenty. If you wouldn't open it, don't send it. If you outgrow Shopify Messaging, Klaviyo and Omnisend are the usual next steps. Both have free plans to start.
Use limited drops and urgency
Create excitement with small batches
details

You already build in small batches. Use that:

  • Say how many: "I built 5 of these. When they're gone, they're gone."
  • Set a time: "Friday at noon ET." It gives people something to wait for.
  • Tease it: Post build progress leading up to the drop.
  • Track inventory so the page shows how many are left.

It isn't fake scarcity. You really only made 5.

Pedal builder tip Email your list and post on Instagram at the same moment the drop goes live.
Collect customer reviews with Judge.me
Social proof helps new customers trust you

Offer bundles and cross-sells
Increase your average order value

Selling one customer two pedals is cheaper than finding two customers:

  • Add-ons: Stickers, patch cables, a power cable. Small stuff adds up.

Product page SEO basics
Help people find your pedals on Google
details

SEO means showing up when people search Google. The basics:

  • Titles: Say what it is. "Thunderclap Overdrive Pedal" beats "Thunderclap."
  • Descriptions: At least 100 words, in normal language, using the words people search for ("overdrive pedal," "handmade," "boutique").
  • Image alt text: Describe each photo ("Thunderclap overdrive guitar pedal, front").
  • Search listing: At the bottom of each product, edit the "Search engine listing" title and description.
Pedal builder tip SEO takes months to kick in. Do the basics now and let it build while you focus on Instagram and email.
Join pedal communities
Build relationships on Reddit, TGP, and Facebook groups
details

The pedal world is small. Being part of it is how people find you:

  • Reddit: r/guitarpedals, r/diypedals, r/letstradepedals. Help people, share builds, answer questions. Don't just post ads.
  • The Gear Page: The Effects forum is full of pedal nerds. Join in before you promote.

Pedal builder tip When someone asks "what overdrive should I get?" and yours really is a good answer, say so, and say you built it. People respect that.
Build repeat customer strategies
Turn one-time buyers into lifelong fans
details

Repeat customers keep a small pedal company alive:

  • Follow up: Two weeks after delivery, send a personal email asking how they like it.
  • Early access: Give past buyers first shot at new drops.
  • Next-order code: Put a small discount code in the order confirmation (e.g., "THANKS10").
  • Handwritten notes: A short thank-you in every box. Costs nothing, people remember it.
  • Reshare: When customers post your pedal on their board, like it, comment, and share it.
Pedal builder tip A note and a sticker cost under 50¢ and get people talking about you.

Check your store on mobile

Pick a plan and remove the password page
Your store is hidden behind a password until you do this
details

New Shopify stores are password-protected so nobody sees them half-built. To open up:

  1. Pick your plan if you haven't yet (Settings > Plan).
  2. Go to Online Store > Preferences.
  3. Under Password protection, uncheck Restrict access with password and save.
